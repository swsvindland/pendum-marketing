## body-hydration

PRICE BANDS (US App Store and Google Play, read 2026-10-03). Weight-trend and body apps: monthly $1.99-$5.99 (Happy Scale $1.99, Libra $1.99, Vekt $1.99, Kosshii $2.99, WeightFit $2.99, Progress $3.99-$5.99, Scelta $4.99, MeThreeSixty $4.99; FormaTrack is the outlier at $12.99); yearly $9.99-$29.99 (Vekt $9.99, Happy Scale $11.99, Progress $11.99-$19.99, Libra $17.99, WeightFit $17.99, Kosshii $19.99, Scelta $29.99, MeThreeSixty $19.99-$39.99); lifetime $29.99-$59.99 (Progress $29.99, Happy Scale $39.99, Kosshii $49.99, Scelta $59.99; FormaTrack $119 is an outlier); paid upfront exists and is cheap: Weight Diary $7.99 with a 4.9 rating and no IAP, Monitor Your Weight free plus a $0.99 sync unlock. Hydration apps: monthly $0.99-$3.99 (Waterllama $0.99-$3.99, WaterMinder $2.99, Hydro Coach $2.99; Plant Nanny $3.99-$7.99 and VGFIT's unnamed ladder to $79.99 are the outliers); yearly $9.99-$29.99 (Wachanga $9.99-$15.49, Hydro Coach $19.99, WaterMinder $19.99 list with $7.49-$14.99 offers; Plant Nanny $69.99 is the outlier); lifetime or one-time $1.99-$44.99 (WaterTrack $1.99 upfront, Daily Water Pro $0.99 upfront, Leap Fitness $2.99 unlock, Simple Daily Water Tracker $5.99, Waterllama about $7 'Full Access', Hydro Coach PRO $10 on Android, Wachanga $19.99, Hydro Coach iOS lifetime $39.99-$44.99; WaterMinder's Android lifetime is somewhere in the $15-$49.99 range and unverified; Plant Nanny and MeThreeSixty have no one-time option at all).

WHERE ONE-TIME PURCHASES EXIST. Nearly every well-rated weight app keeps a lifetime tier (Happy Scale, Progress, Kosshii, Scelta, FormaTrack) and the best-rated hydration app (Waterllama, 4.9 from 162K) sells a roughly $7 one-time unlock next to its $0.99 monthly. Hydro Coach sells a $10 PRO app on Android and a $39.99-$44.99 lifetime on iOS. Pure paid-upfront apps are rare and small (WaterTrack $1.99, Daily Water Pro $0.99, Weight Diary $7.99) but Weight Diary proves a $7.99 upfront weight-trend app can hold a 4.9 rating. Apple Watch support is common in hydration (WaterMinder, Waterllama, Plant Nanny, VGFIT, Daily Water Pro, WaterTrack) and in subscription weight apps (Vekt, WeightFit, Scelta, Kosshii, Monitor Your Weight) but absent from Happy Scale, Progress, Libra and Weight Diary; Wear OS is offered by WaterMinder and Leap Fitness.

GAPS A ONE-TIME, OFFLINE, NO-ACCOUNT APP CAN FILL. 1) A weight-trend app with tape measurements and progress photos at one price: today photos are the paywalled feature everywhere (Progress Pro, WeightFit Premium, FormaTrack 'Unlimited photos' $3.99, Kosshii Premium), and Happy Scale, the trend leader, has no measurements, photos, Android or Watch. A $9.99-$14.99 one-time price sits between Weight Diary ($7.99 upfront) and the $29.99-$39.99 lifetime tiers while undercutting every yearly plan after year one. 2) A hydration app without ads, trials or an account: the leaders either run ads in the free tier (Hydro Coach, WaterMinder, Plant Nanny, Leap) or hide the one-time option behind generic IAP names. Waterllama's roughly $7 one-time unlock and Hydro Coach PRO's $10 define the band; $4.99-$9.99 one-time with Apple Watch and Health sync is the defensible spot (WaterTrack at $1.99 shows price alone brings no traction, so the sale has to be the Watch app, the export and the no-account promise). 3) Say explicitly what the competitors are criticised for: history and export never paywalled, no account, no trial, no ads, no renewal, and no feature later moved behind a subscription. 4) Family pricing: no competitor sells a one-time bundle of weight, hydration, lifting and macros; a four-app one-time bundle below the $39.99-$59.99 single-app lifetime tiers has no comparable offer. 5) Android is underserved for weight trend: Happy Scale is iOS-only and Libra is the ad-funded, cloud-account default there. WHERE ONE-TIME PURCHASES EXIST. Pure paid-upfront apps are now rare and priced per platform: Things 3 $9.99 iPhone, $19.99 iPad, $49.99 Mac; Reeder Classic $4.99. Most 'one-time' options are non-consumable unlocks inside a free app sold next to a subscription: Halide $69.99 (3.5x its $19.99 year), Darkroom $99.99 (2.5x its $39.99 year, with a legacy $9.99 unlock preserved), Flighty $299 (5x its $59.99 year), Hevy $74.99 (3.1x), Strong $99.99 (3.3x). Rule of thumb in the data: lifetime = 2.5–5x the annual price, and lifetime is the one SKU vendors never discount (Hevy) or discount only on promotion (Strong $79.99). No nutrition or body-composition tracker in the set sells a one-time unlock at all. The indie apps that work with no account are exactly the ones with a one-time or iCloud-only model (Things, Reeder Classic, Bear, Halide, Darkroom, Carrot); every one of the ten fitness apps requires an account, and none advertises offline use for the phone app (Hevy advertises it only for the watch).

PLATFORM MECHANICS (verified 2026-10-03 unless dated). Apple price points: 800 by default plus 100 higher ones on request up to $10,000 (App Store Connect Help, 'Set a price'); the full ladder runs $0.29–$10,000 in $0.10 steps under $10 and $0.50 steps $10–$50 (9to5mac, 2022-12-06; in force for apps and IAP since spring 2023). You pick one base storefront whose price Apple never touches; the other 174 storefronts are auto-equalised for FX and tax, and Apple announces those adjustments in Developer News (2026-01-29 and 2026-08-27 posts: VAT changes in Mauritius, Morocco, Congo etc.; auto-renewable subscriptions and manually managed prices are excluded). Storefront prices are consumer prices with VAT included where Apple is merchant of record and remits tax (Paid Applications Agreement Exhibit B, per the 2026-08-27 post); proceeds are net of that tax and the commission. Paid-app price changes can be global, temporary (up to one year, per storefront) or custom per storefront. Small Business Program: 15% commission when the prior calendar year's proceeds across all associated accounts were at most $1M; new developers qualify; you leave the year after exceeding it and can re-qualify. Subscriptions drop to 15% after one year of paid service for everyone; trials do not count toward that year. Google Play: 15% on the first $1M per year (enrolment in an account group is required), 30% above, 15% on auto-renewing subscriptions regardless of revenue; the fees page now also lists a regional model effective 2026-06-30 (EEA, UK, US) and 2026-09-30 (Australia, Japan) of 10% plus a 5% billing fee for subscriptions and for other transactions on new installs, 20% plus 5% for other transactions on existing installs; confirm on the live page before quoting it. App bundles (Apple): 2–10 apps from one developer, either all paid (bundle must cost less than the sum; Complete My Bundle credits apps already owned) or all free with an approved auto-renewable subscription in each app that unlocks every app in the bundle; the two kinds cannot mix, and a free app with a non-consumable unlock does not qualify, so four free Vector apps with one-time unlocks cannot be bundled, while four paid-upfront apps can, and bundles cannot be pre-ordered. Pre-orders (Apple): free or paid apps, 2–180 days ahead for a first release (2–365 for new regions); the customer is charged on release day at the lower of the pre-order price and the release-day price; IAP cannot be pre-ordered and promoted IAP stays hidden until release; a region can be pre-ordered only once. Google Play pre-registration: for apps and games, recommended 3–6 weeks before launch, optional auto-install and launch rewards; the page does not say whether paid apps are supported. Introductory offers (Apple): free trial 3 days to 1 year, pay-as-you-go 1–12 periods, pay-up-front 1, 2, 3, 6 or 12 months; one per subscription group per customer; can differ by storefront; cannot be edited once created; promotional, win-back and offer-code offers exist for returning subscribers. Google Play: free trials 3 days to 3 years, intro pricing as a single payment or 1–52 recurrences. Non-subscription free trials (Review Guideline 3.1.1) must state the duration, what expires and the charge before they start. Subscription price increases (Apple): you may preserve the old price for existing subscribers; consent is required if the region demands it, if the rise exceeds 50% and about $5 per period or $50 per year, or if there was a rise in the past 12 months; notice is 27 days for monthly and 60 days for annual; one scheduled change per storefront; decreases apply automatically. Google Play: increases need opt-in with at least 30 days' notice unless the opt-out path applies (at most 50% or 17 cents per day, once per base plan per region per year); legacy price cohorts are supported. Lifetime IAP: Apple's non-consumable type is restorable, can be Family Shared, and Guideline 3.1.2(a) explicitly allows one-time purchases alongside subscriptions and forbids removing functionality people already paid for when a model changes; Guideline 2.3.2 requires IAP to be disclosed in the listing. Google Play one-time (managed) products carry the ordinary transaction fee, not the subscription rate.

Noom: billing rigidity, cancellation disputes and dynamic pricing (Trustpilot summary 2026-10-02; nutrola 2026-04-06). Hevy: free-tier limits rewritten 27 times (getpulsesignal, last change 2026-09-30), so the free tier is a moving target. GAPS A ONE-TIME, OFFLINE, NO-ACCOUNT APP COULD FILL. (a) Nothing in the fitness set runs without an account; the no-account, works-offline promise is unique in this comparison and is the same promise that Things, Bear, Halide and Darkroom sell. (b) No mainstream nutrition, hydration or body-composition tracker offers a one-time price; only workout loggers do, at $74.99–$99.99. (c) Free tiers in this category are capped on routines and exercises rather than time-limited, so a time-limited or feature-limited trial that converts to a permanent unlock is a different, clearer promise. (d) Annual subscriptions in the coaching band cost $72–$100 per year and the loggers $24–$30; a one-time unlock in the $9.99–$29.99 range per app undercuts every subscription after year one and every lifetime SKU on day one. (e) Sellers with lifetime SKUs never discount them, which makes a stable published one-time price a trust signal in itself. (f) Store mechanics favour 'free app + non-consumable unlock' for discoverability, trials and Family Sharing, but only paid-upfront apps can be sold as an App Store bundle; the owner's open 'core unlock vs paid download' decision therefore also decides whether a four-app bundle is possible. Evidence-based ranges for the owner's decision, not decisions: one-time core unlock per Vector app $9.99–$19.99 (one month of Fitbod or MyFitnessPal, a quarter of the Hevy lifetime), with the same price on both stores and at most one trial mechanism; if paid upfront instead, $9.99–$19.99 per app with a discounted paid bundle and pre-orders; for OmegaFyt Pro, $4.99–$9.99/mo and $39.99–$59.99/yr brackets the RevenueCat medians ($9.99 / $39.94) between the loggers and the coaching apps, with a 7-day trial as the category norm and annual promoted first (68% of category sales). GAPS A ONE-TIME, OFFLINE, NO-ACCOUNT APP CAN FILL. 1) A maintained, well-designed, pay-once tracker between $9.99 and $19.99: above the $1.99 to $4.99 indie floor (which signals abandonware) and equal to one to two months of any mainstream subscription or a quarter to a half of one year; $14.99 (Caltopia) is the current ceiling for paid-upfront, and $29.99 to $39.99 is the band buyers already accept for 'lifetime' inside Calory and Stupid Simple, so a $19.99 to $29.99 core unlock also has precedent if the app is clearly richer than the indies. 2) Offline and no account as a first-class claim: among the large apps only MyNetDiary markets both, and it is still a subscription; Cronometer and MacroFactor have said they cannot or will not. 3) Free what others paywall: barcode scanning, custom macro targets, Health sync, export and no ads are the features most often cited as gated. 4) Honest database copy: subscription vendors justify recurring fees with hosted databases, so an offline app must state what is bundled (Caltopia says 48,000 USDA foods; Nutrients about 200,000) and how it updates. 5) Predictable cost: 'buy once, no renewal, no price increase' is the message the one-time segment is converging on and matches the owner's model; a family-of-apps bundle has a precedent in MacroFactor's $89.99 nutrition plus workouts bundle. 6) Android remains almost empty of polished one-time trackers (Intake at about 1.5K installs, Waistline free), so the Vector line is not fenced in by a strong incumbent there either."

Price increases are endemic and visible as '(Legacy Pricing)' SKUs: Fitbod $12.99/$79.99 to $15.99/$95.99 (2026), Strong $3.99/$23.99/$79.99 to $4.99/$29.99/$99.99, JEFIT yearly $34.99 to $69.99; secondary sources (thepricer.org 2026-09-24, sensai.fit 2026-09-08) and a Strong Google Play review (2024-10-24, lifetime 'expired despite being lifetime') show the trust cost. Free tiers cap routines (Strong 3, Hevy 4, Gym Log+ 4), custom exercises (Hevy 7), history (Hevy 3 months) or stored workouts (Liftin' 5/month, Forma 8, HeavySet routine runs); trials are 7 days (Fitbod card-required, Boostcamp annual, Setgraph, StrongLifts yearly) or 14 days (Alpha, Juggernaut, Benchmark), and several vendors refuse monthly trials outright (StrongLifts: 'to discourage it'; Boostcamp, Alpha). Accounts: Strong (help article: all users prompted to sign up), Hevy (data lives on Hevy's servers, not the device), Fitbod, JEFIT, RP, JuggernautAI and Gravitus are account-centred; Alpha Progression is the only large app that is no-account and fully offline, and it is still subscription-only. Offline support is partial and caveated (Fitbod: local algorithm but history and videos need a connection; Boostcamp: after a program loads; Hevy: only an unsaved workout survives; RP: web app, 'unusable when offline' per its Play page). Complaints, dated and first-hand where possible: basic functions paywalled (Boostcamp review 2025-02-04), cancellation requiring account deletion (JuggernautAI Play review), repeated price rises and hard cancellation (Fitbod, 2026 secondary sources), platform parity for the same price (Setgraph Android review 2026-05-28), and praise for the opposite: 'a one time purchase option' (Hevy reviewer 2025-07-26), 'worth dropping $5 on to remove ads' (SelahSoft 2026-07-31), 'the majority seem to require a subscription for one essential feature or another' (FitNotes Supporter reviewer). Makers of IronRep, Benchmark and Gym Log+ now market explicitly against 'subscription fatigue' with 'no account, no server'. Reddit threads could not be read from this environment (blocked at every route; search budget exhausted), so that sentiment is attested here only through the store reviews and the maker statements above; the one located thread (r/Hevy, 2023) is old. Gaps a one-time, offline, no-account app can fill: (a) every no-account/offline lifting app found is a single-developer iOS app with under 1k ratings, or an Android app with trivial distribution (Hardlog 10+ downloads) or a dated UI (FitNotes, SelahSoft); nobody ships a maintained, designed, no-account, offline tracker on both iOS and Android; (b) the big trackers' lifetime tiers ($74.99–$99.99) still bind data to an account and server, so 'own it, keep it on the phone, export any time' is an open position; (c) a one-time price of $14.99–$29.99 per app undercuts one year of Fitbod/JEFIT/Alpha ($69.99–$95.99) and three months of RP/Juggernaut, while sitting above the $0.99–$4.99 hobby tier that signals abandonment risk; a family bundle across the four apps has no comparator at all; (d) stating the free limit in plain numbers before download (routines, history, workouts) and never using 'legacy' SKUs would directly answer the most common complaints; (e) offline and no-account claims should be specific (what syncs via Health, what OS backups include), because the competitors' offline claims are caveated and reviewers notice.

### Model

Recommendation: a mixed family, chosen per app by what a trial can show and what the store mechanics cost. Vector Lift and Vector Macros: free download with a one-time core unlock (App Store non-consumable, Google Play one-time product) and a 21-day full trial. Vector Body and Vector Hydration: paid-upfront downloads with no trial and no billing code. Trial mechanics, corrected from the first proposal: on iOS the trial is Apple's own mechanism under Guideline 3.1.1 (read 2026-10-03): a non-consumable at Price Tier 0 named "21-day Trial", whose signed transaction purchase date starts the clock, survives reinstall because it is tied to the Apple ID, and is the product App Review expects to see; the sheet must state the duration, what stops working and the $19.99 charge before the trial starts. Starting it is an App Store sheet on first launch, a friction the risks list carries. On Android one-time products cannot be free, so the start date is local and a reinstall can reset it; accept that leak rather than add a server. 21 days, not 14, because Macros holds its first adaptive check-in until 12 covered days and six weigh-ins in the last 21 and Lift's volume-follows-recovery needs a second week, so a 14-day trial could not show the feature each price rests on. The never-locked-records rule is now stated per app and only where the fact sheet supports it: a lapsed Lift or Macros still opens, reads, charts, backs up and exports (both ship encrypted backups and CSV); only new entries need the unlock. Body and Hydration have no export today, so the rule is not claimed for them, and as paid-upfront apps nothing in them lapses; CSV export for weight and drink logs is recommended as a launch gate for both, but the prices do not depend on it. Trade-offs, with the evidence. Trials: neither store offers a trial for a paid-upfront app (Weight Diary needs a separate Lite listing), while Benchmark ($14.99 unlock, two-week trial) is the precedent for a free app with a timed trial, and RevenueCat's data says a hard paywall converts about 5x better than open freemium at day 35 (10.7 vs 2.1 percent); a trial matters most for Macros, where asking $19.99 upfront for a 1 GB app with no ratings and no way to try the catalog would be the weakest offer in the category, and least for Hydration, where every leader logs water free for ever and the complaint the category hands out is 'paywall'. Bundles: only an all-paid-upfront family can form an App Store bundle; the mixed model forgoes it, and Android has no bundles either way. Pre-orders: Apple pre-orders free or paid apps (2 to 180 days ahead) and the customer pays the lower of the pre-order and release-day price; Apple in-app purchases cannot be pre-ordered and stay hidden until release. Google Play, corrected: Billing Library 8.0.0 (2025-06-30) added multiple purchase options and offers for one-time products and 8.1.0 (2025-11-06) added pre-order APIs for them (release notes read 2026-10-03), so the Play unlock could carry an offer or be pre-ordered; whether the Expo apps can use that depends on the purchase library (react-native-purchases or expo-iap) exposing Billing Library 8.1, and with no launch discount there is nothing to lock in, so it is not relied on. Refunds: both stores refund at their discretion; a refunded non-consumable is revoked the next time StoreKit 2 sees the revocation (Transaction.currentEntitlements), a refunded paid app stays installed, and on Android a one-time product is voided by Play Billing on the next online query while the self-service refund window on paid apps invites try-and-refund; no server is needed, which keeps the Macros plan's requirement that ordinary offline use never waits on validation. Piracy: negligible on iOS under both models; on Android a paid APK is shareable in minutes and a local unlock is patchable in an hour, so neither model wins and the no-account design rules out server licensing; accept it. Reviews: free apps earn the installs and the ratings that come with them (Waterllama 162K at about $7, HeavySet 1.3k at $19.99) while paid-upfront fitness apps are tiny (Repline, Caltopia, Calometric, PayOnce with 1 rating), but Weight Diary (4.9, 2.6K) shows a paid weight app can be rated well; paid-upfront draws 'too expensive' one-stars, free-plus-unlock draws 'paywall' one-stars, and the per-app never-locked rule answers the second where it applies. Cost: paid-upfront needs no billing code and could ship today; free-plus-unlock needs StoreKit 2 and Play Billing, restore, the Tier 0 trial product, trial state and the unlock sheet, which the fact sheets confirm exist in none of the four apps; the mixed model writes that code twice, not four times, and exercises it on Lift before Macros. Direction of change later, corrected: paid to free-plus-unlock is possible on both stores (on iOS grandfather by AppTransaction.originalPurchaseDate; on Android a paid app has no in-app receipt, so distinguishing earlier buyers needs the legacy Licensing Verification Library, an online check cached on the first online launch after the migration with a grace path for users who never go online, which conflicts with the offline promise, so plan any such migration for iOS only or accept the LVL check). Free to paid is possible on the App Store only: Google Play states that once an app has been offered free it can never be changed to paid (help 6334373, read 2026-10-03), so free-plus-unlock on Play for Lift and Macros is final, and paid-upfront for Body and Hydration keeps the reversible direction open. Verdict: free-plus-unlock with a 21-day Tier 0 trial for Lift and Macros, paid-upfront for Body and Hydration; one honest site line covers both: buy once, no account, no renewal. Fallback if the owner wants all four out before any billing code exists: paid-upfront at the same four prices with the $39.99 App Store bundle and pre-orders at list price, migrating Lift and Macros to free-plus-unlock later on iOS, and on Android only if the LVL grandfathering path is accepted.

### Rationale

Anchoring. The research gives three bands for every category: subscriptions (loggers $2.99 to $4.99/mo, nutrition $8.99 to $14.99/mo, coaching $11.99 to $34.99/mo), lifetime unlocks inside those apps (2.5 to 5x the annual price: Hevy $74.99, Strong $99.99, Calory $34.99, Happy Scale $39.99, Progress $29.99, Halide $69.99), and paid-upfront indies ($0.99 to $14.99, nearly all unmaintained or unrated). The Vector prices sit in the gap between the second and third bands, where all four reports say nobody maintained ships: $19.99, $19.99, $9.99 and $4.99 are each at or above the top of the indie band and at or below a third of the nearest lifetime SKU, so the same number reads as serious against the indies and cheap against the lifetimes. Every price is a standard Apple price point so Apple's 174-storefront equalisation applies without manual prices; Play prices are set from the US price by its regional templates. The family order now follows what the buyer gets, not cost to maintain: Lift and Macros each carry an engine (set prescription with a Watch companion; adaptive check-ins with the catalog), Body and Hydration are single-purpose. The ladder loses one $5 step by putting Lift level with Macros; it gains a price that never has to rise. OmegaFyt is priced as the account-and-sync option, not the premium one: $49.99/yr is under every coaching app, and the Lift plus Macros pair ($39.98 once) costs less than one OmegaFyt year, which the site can state as 'own it' against 'sync it' with the feature axis beside it. Net of fees: 15 percent under Apple's Small Business Program (which only takes effect 15 days after the end of the fiscal month in which enrolment is approved, so enrolment must be approved a full month before release) and, on Play, 10 percent plus a 5 percent billing fee on new installs in the EEA, UK and US from 2026-06-30 (Australia and Japan from 2026-09-30) and the 15 percent tier elsewhere, $19.99 nets about $16.99 and $49.99/yr about $42.49 before VAT differences. Objections accepted from the critique: Play's free-is-permanent rule and the one-way fallback; export promised only where it ships; the Tier 0 'XX-day Trial' product and the reinstall leak being Android-only; one discount rule everywhere (none); Play Billing 8.0 offers and 8.1 pre-orders; 21-day trials; photo logging as a hardware-gated bonus and 'two on-device models' corrected to the phone's own Apple Intelligence or Gemini Nano models; the 66x line dropped and Caltopia marked unverified; Lift to $19.99; no public launch window; Hydration paid-upfront (and Body with it, my extension of the same argument); the Android grandfathering mechanism named; fee timing and the Play regional model; the downward OmegaFyt test; the wording nits (annual subscribers switch at renewal, a monthly subscriber has spent the pair's price in five months; Watch companion plus iPhone Live Activity; Body lacks export); and Family Sharing treated as permanent, bundle pre-order as unavailable and offer codes for non-consumables as unverified (Apple announced offer codes for one-time purchases in 2025, but the App Store Connect help pages returned 404 today, so verify in App Store Connect and record the URL and date in docs/sources before use). Objections rejected or narrowed: (1) keeping a $14.99 launch price for Lift as 'its only concession', because any public price that moves is the review the plan is built to avoid and Apple's pre-order mechanics cannot lock an in-app price anyway; the launch concession is codes, not a window. (2) Using Cal AI $29.99 as an OmegaFyt list-price anchor; it is accepted only as the reason the first test probes $39.99, since Cal AI is a server-side scanner with A/B paywalls and no coaching. (3) Making CSV export a condition of the prices; it is a launch gate for the per-app promise, and the prices stand without it. (4) '21 days everywhere for one sentence on the site': with Body and Hydration paid-upfront there is no trial to describe for them, so the site has one sentence per model instead. All competitor prices are US storefront list prices read on 2026-10-03; Reddit could not be read by any of the four researchers, so complaint evidence is store reviews, vendor help pages, Trustpilot and Hacker News.

### Risks

- Too cheap: Hydration at $4.99 and Body at $9.99 net about $4.24 and $8.49 a sale, so they are ratings and brand builders, not revenue; WaterTrack at $1.99 with one rating shows a low price buys nothing on its own. Mitigation: hold the prices, never discount them, and let Macros and Lift carry revenue.
- Too expensive: Macros and Lift at $19.99 each face free tiers (MyFitnessPal, Hevy, Strong), Caltopia at $14.99 and Benchmark at $14.99, with zero-rating listings and, for Macros, an Android install of about twice the 561 MiB catalog. Sensitivity is measured, not guessed: judge each app at 90 days on revenue per install and the iOS-to-Android unlock ratio over the same window (the RevenueCat 10.7 percent figure is a subscription hard-paywall benchmark, not a one-time-unlock trigger, and is dropped). No price moves before 12 months; a $24.99 Macros test waits for 500 ratings.
- Photo logging unavailable on my phone: Macros' photo logging needs Apple Intelligence on eligible iPhones (photos need iOS 27) or Gemini Nano on supported Android phones, has not run on an Android device, and no fallback model ships. Buyers on an iPhone 14 or a midrange Android will refund and one-star if the sheet or the site sells it unqualified. Mitigation: price on catalog, check-ins and scanning; state the device requirement on the unlock sheet and the site; never lead with photo logging.
- Trial friction and leaks: on iOS the 21-day trial is a $0 App Store purchase, so the first launch shows a store sheet before the app is usable, and a user who declines sees a locked app; on Android the trial start is local and a reinstall resets it. Accept both rather than add a server or an account. App Review rejects a trial that lacks the Tier 0 '21-day Trial' product or whose sheet omits duration, what stops and the price (Guideline 3.1.1).
- Google Play free listings are permanent: once Lift and Macros ship free on Play they can never become paid apps (help 6334373). Body and Hydration ship paid, which can later go free but not back. Decide the Play model per app as a one-way door.
- No trial on Body and Hydration: paid-upfront apps cannot be tried on either store, and Weight Diary needed a Lite listing to get around it. Mitigation: screenshots and the site's calculators carry the demonstration; a Lite listing is not planned.
- Cannibalising OmegaFyt: Lift plus Macros is $39.98 once against $49.99 a year. An annual subscriber switches at renewal; a monthly subscriber at $7.99 has spent the pair's price in five months. Accept it: Vector sales carry no server cost and OmegaFyt pays FatSecret, Fly.io, Clerk and PostHog, so a switched customer is not lost margin. Keep the two offers separated by need (account, web, cross-device sync, server food search, supplements, soreness, steps against own it, offline, no account), never bundle OmegaFyt with the Vector apps, never sell an OmegaFyt lifetime, and never show a price-only comparison without the feature axis.
- Android willingness to pay: Android one-time prices run lower (Hydro Coach PRO $10 on Android against $39.99 to $44.99 lifetime on iOS; Heft $1.99 on Android against $44.99 on iOS; Simple Workout Log PRO Key $4.99; Intake at about 1.5K installs) and Setgraph's Android review complains about paying the same for less. Launch at parity (one price on the site, one promise), review at 12 months, and lower Android, if at all, in one permanent step announced as the new price. Android Lift also has no recorded device QA and Macros' photo and label scanning have not run on an Android device; do not ship Android builds the fact sheets say are untested.
- Price rises as a trust cost: the one complaint every report shares is the rise (Fitbod to $15.99/$95.99, Strong to $4.99/$99.99, JEFIT to $69.99/yr, Lose It $9.99 to $23.99, Lifesum to about EUR 9.99). This plan has no public price movement at all: no launch window, no sales, no regional cuts, no store weeks, for at least 12 months, and never a 'legacy' label. For OmegaFyt, preserve the existing price for current subscribers on any rise (Apple allows it; consent is forced above 50 percent or about $50 a year, notice 27 days monthly and 60 days annual; Play needs opt-in with 30 or 60 days' notice depending on the region unless the rise is under 50 percent and 17 cents a day).
- The word 'lifetime': Strong's 'lifetime expired' review and Hydro Coach's lost Pro purchase show the liability. Call the SKU 'Unlock' or 'Buy once', say what it covers (every feature in this app, no renewal) and nothing about future apps; Guideline 3.1.2(a) already forbids removing paid functionality.
- Export and backup gaps: Body and Hydration have no backup or export, so the site cannot promise 'take your records with you' for them, and a lost phone loses the records. Ship CSV export for weight and drink logs before claiming it; keep the never-locked-records line to Lift and Macros.
- A/B tests as a complaint in themselves: Cal AI's tested paywalls and Noom's dynamic pricing are cited grievances. Test OmegaFyt prices only on new users, one variable at a time, for a bounded period, and never show a tested price on the site.
- Fees and enrolment timing: Apple's 15 percent applies only from 15 days after the end of the fiscal month in which Small Business Program enrolment is approved, so a launch month with late enrolment pays 30 percent; Play's regional model (10 percent plus 5 percent billing fee on new installs in the EEA, UK and US from 2026-06-30) needs no enrolment, and the 15 percent tier elsewhere is automatic under $1M; confirm both live pages before quoting a net figure.
- Family Sharing and Family Library: Apple non-consumables and paid apps can be Family Shared, and App Store Connect help on whether sharing can be turned off again returned 404 today, so treat turning it on as permanent; Play Family Library covers paid apps (Body, Hydration) but not one-time in-app products (Lift, Macros), so the two stores and the two models differ and the site must not promise sharing without checking each case.
- Catalog storage: a free Macros download of about twice the 561 MiB catalog on Android invites 'too big' reviews from trial users who never buy; Play Asset Delivery install-time packs are needed to ship it at all, and the fact sheet still lacks measured download and install sizes.
- Unverified anchors on the site: Caltopia's App Store page was never located and several competitor figures are second-hand; nothing goes on the pricing page without its URL and date in docs/sources, and the page must carry its read date because every listed price has changed within two years.

### Launch tactics

- Launch at the final prices with no launch window: $19.99 Macros, $19.99 Lift, $9.99 Body, $4.99 Hydration, printed once on the site and never moved for 12 months. The only launch concession is codes that leave the public price alone: Google Play promo codes for the one-time products and paid apps (press, newsletter, early testers), and Apple offer codes for the non-consumables if App Store Connect confirms they cover one-time purchases (announced 2025; the help page returned 404 today, so verify and record before use). App Store promo codes for the two paid apps are available in any case.
- Pre-order and pre-registration: pre-order all four on the App Store (2 to 180 days ahead; Body and Hydration at list price, Lift and Macros free with the unlock and the Tier 0 trial hidden until release) and use Play pre-registration (3 to 6 weeks ahead, auto-install on) so day-one installs land together for chart position. Do not rely on Play's one-time product pre-order (Billing Library 8.1) until the Expo purchase library is confirmed to expose it; with no discount there is nothing to lock in.
- Stagger by size and by model: ship Hydration and Body first (paid-upfront, no billing code, so they exercise App Review, the listings and the site's store badges), then Lift (first app with StoreKit 2, Play Billing, the '21-day Trial' Tier 0 product, restore and the unlock sheet), then Macros, so a rejection or a billing bug costs the least and the catalog-size work finishes last.
- Ask for a rating only after the unlock in Lift and Macros (SKStoreReviewController and Play In-App Review), never during the trial or on a lapsed app; in Body and Hydration ask after two weeks of entries. The paid comparator with the best rating (Weight Diary 4.9) is rated by buyers.
- Family Sharing: turn it on for the two non-consumables in App Store Connect only once its reversibility is confirmed (treat it as permanent); paid apps share by default. Say on the site only what each store actually does: Play Family Library covers the two paid apps, not the two unlocks.
- Enrol in the Apple Small Business Program so that approval lands at least one full fiscal month before the first release (proceeds adjust 15 days after the end of the month of approval). On Play no enrolment is needed for the regional 10 percent plus 5 percent model; join an account group for the 15 percent tier in other markets.
- Cross-sell inside the apps: a Settings row 'Other Vector apps' linking to the store pages, and the site's calculators linking to the matching app, which is where the traffic is today. No Play sale week, no regional cuts, no store-specific offers in year one.
- OmegaFyt at launch: products pro_monthly_799 and pro_annual_4999 in one subscription group, 7-day free trial as an introductory offer on the annual product only (Apple: one intro offer per subscription group per customer; Play: a free-trial offer on the annual base plan). Promote annual first on the paywall. Prices live in App Store Connect, Google Play and RevenueCat, never in code.
- OmegaFyt A/B in RevenueCat: use RevenueCat Experiments rather than the PostHog offering flag for price tests, so assignment, conversion and revenue per paywall view are computed in one place (keep the PostHog flag as a kill switch). Create the extra store products first (pro_annual_3999, and a 14-day-trial variant of the annual) and put each in its own offering. Test 1: annual $39.99 against $49.99 with monthly held at $7.99, new users only, one variable, four weeks or about 1,000 paywall views per arm, judged on revenue per paywall view, not conversion rate. Test 2, on the winner: 7-day against 14-day trial. Test $59.99 only if $49.99 wins test 1. Existing subscribers keep their product and price throughout, and no tested price ever appears on the site.
- After six months of OmegaFyt data, add Apple win-back offers and Play lapsed-subscriber offers (for example two months at $3.99) instead of cutting list prices.
- Hold the one-time prices for at least 12 months. If Android pricing is ever changed, change it once, as a permanent new price, announced in advance, never as a sale; the research notes that sellers never discount their lifetime SKUs and that a published, unchanging one-time price is itself the trust signal.

### Objections the review raised

Verdict: The price ladder ($19.99 / $14.99 / $9.99 / $4.99, OmegaFyt $7.99 and $49.99 with no lifetime) is consistent with the research and with the one-time, offline, no-account model, and every competitor figure checks against the four research files. The proposal fails on four points that must be fixed before anything reaches the owner table: (1) it says free-to-paid is allowed later, but Google Play makes a free app permanently free (support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6334373), so the "direction of change" and the paid-first fallback are one-way on Android; (2) the never-locked-records rule promises "exporting", which Vector Hydration and Vector Body cannot do today (app-facts.json: no application-level backup or export for Hydration, no cloud backup or export for Body); (3) Apple's own trial mechanism under Guideline 3.1.1 is a Tier 0 non-consumable named "XX-day Trial", which the proposal omits and which also invalidates its "reinstall resets the trial" risk on iOS; (4) the plan contradicts itself on discounts (a 30-day launch price, a Play four-app sale week and a 90-day Android cut sit next to "never run a sale in year one" and "hold prices 12 months"). Two platform facts are stale: Play Billing Library 8.0 (2025-06-30) added offers for one-time products and 8.1 (2025-11-06) added pre-orders for them, so "in-app purchases cannot be pre-ordered" is Apple-only. Medium findings: a 14-day trial cannot show Macros' first weekly check-in (the app holds check-ins until 12 covered days and 6 weigh-ins in 21), Macros' photo logging is hardware-gated and cannot carry the $19.99 case unqualified, the "66 times larger catalog" line compares 48,000 USDA foods with 3.2 million packaged entries while Vector has 7,793 USDA foods, Lift at Benchmark's exact price leaves money on the table for the only app in the family with a prescription engine and a Watch app, and Small Business Program proceeds only adjust 15 days after the end of the month enrolment is approved. Recommend: keep the ladder, raise Lift to $19.99 or keep the launch price as its only concession, drop every discount except one pre-announced launch window (or none), make Hydration paid-upfront, ship CSV export in Body and Hydration before promising it, and rewrite the platform paragraph with the Play facts above.

- [high] The model section says 'free to paid is also allowed as long as existing owners keep what they bought (Guideline 3.1.2(a))'. That is Apple only. Google Play: 'You can change your app from paid to free. Once your app has been offered for free, the app can't be changed to paid' (requires a new package name). The fallback path (paid-upfront now, free-plus-unlock after the first quarter) is therefore a one-way door on Android, and the reverse experiment is impossible there. Change: Rewrite 'Direction of change later' as: paid to free-plus-unlock is possible on both stores; free to paid is possible on the App Store only, and on Google Play a free listing is permanent. State that choosing free-plus-unlock on Play is final, and that the paid-upfront fallback can migrate to free later but never back.
- [high] The never-locked-records rule, repeated in the owner table, promises that a lapsed app 'still opens, reads, charts and exports'. Vector Hydration has 'no application-level backup or export' and Vector Body has 'no cloud backup' and no export feature in its fact sheet (only Lift and Macros ship encrypted backups and CSV). The site could not say 'exports' for two of the four apps. Change: Either make CSV export (at least weight and drink logs) a launch gate for Body and Hydration, or word the rule per app: 'opens, reads and charts' for all four, 'and exports' only where the fact sheet shows export. Do not put 'export' in the family-wide promise until all four ship it.
- [high] The proposal says a free app 'can run a time-limited trial under Guideline 3.1.1 if it states the duration, what expires and the charge', and lists 'reinstall can reset the trial' as an accepted risk. Guideline 3.1.1 actually prescribes the mechanism: 'Non-subscription apps may offer a free time-based trial period before presenting a full unlock option by setting up a Non-Consumable IAP item at Price Tier 0 that follows the naming convention: "XX-day Trial."' A Tier 0 non-consumable is a StoreKit transaction tied to the Apple ID, so its purchase date survives reinstall and the trial-reset leak is an Android-only problem. Omitting the Tier 0 product is the likelier App Review rejection, not the missing disclosure. Change: Add to the model: on iOS the trial is a $0 non-consumable named '14-day Trial' (or '21-day Trial', see the check-in objection) whose Transaction.purchaseDate starts the clock; on Android, where one-time products cannot be free, the start date is local and the reinstall leak is accepted. Note the first-launch friction (an App Store sheet to start the trial) in the risks.
- [high] Discount rules contradict each other. Launch tactics say 'Never run a sale on the Vector unlocks in year one' and the owner table says 'never discounted in year one', yet the same proposal has (a) a 30-day launch price 25 to 33 percent under list, (b) 'on Play run a four-app sale week instead' of a bundle, and (c) 'drop Android [Lift] to $9.99' at 90 days, inside the 12-month hold. The research's own trust argument is that lifetime SKUs are never discounted (Hevy) and that price movement is the top complaint. Change: Pick one rule and apply it everywhere: at most one pre-announced launch window on both stores, then no sales, no regional cuts and no store-specific weeks for 12 months. Delete the Play sale week and the 90-day Android cut; if Android pricing is to be reviewed, review it at 12 months and announce any change as a permanent new price.
- [medium] 'In-app purchases cannot be pre-ordered and stay hidden until release' and 'confirm current support for one-time product offers' are stale for Google Play. Play Billing Library 8.0.0 (2025-06-30): 'Multiple purchase options and offers for one-time products'; 8.1.0 (2025-11-06): 'Enabled pre-order APIs for one-time product'. Under free-plus-unlock the Play unlock can carry a discount offer and can be pre-ordered, which is exactly the locked-in launch price the proposal says neither store allows. Change: Rewrite the pre-order and offers sentences as Apple-only. For Play, note that one-time products support offers (Billing Library 8.0) and pre-order (8.1), and that using them depends on the purchase library in the Expo apps (RevenueCat or react-native-iap) exposing Billing Library 8.1; keep 'confirm before relying on it' for that dependency, not for Play itself.
- [medium] A 14-day trial cannot demonstrate the feature Macros' price rests on. The fact sheet says check-ins 'hold until there are at least 12 covered days and six weigh-in days in the last 21', so a trial user who logs every day sees the first adaptive check-in on day 12 at the earliest and most see none. Lift's 'Volume that follows recovery' likewise needs a second week. The RevenueCat 10.7 percent benchmark cited as the Android price-cut trigger is a subscription hard-paywall figure, not a one-time-unlock trial figure, so the trigger is arbitrary. Change: Make the Macros and Lift trials 21 days ('21-day Trial' Tier 0 product) so one check-in and one progression week fall inside it; keep 14 for Body and Hydration or use 21 everywhere for one sentence on the site. Replace the 10.7 percent trigger with revenue per install and the iOS-to-Android conversion ratio measured over the same 90 days.
- [medium] Macros' $19.99 case leans on photo logging against Cal AI, but photo logging 'only works on phones with Apple Intelligence (photos need iOS 27 on eligible iPhones) or Gemini Nano on supported Android phones', has not run on an Android device, and no downloadable fallback model ships. A buyer on an iPhone 14 or a midrange Android pays $19.99 for an unlock whose headline feature their phone cannot run, which is a refund and one-star driver the risks list omits. 'Two on-device models' in the rationale is also wrong: the app uses the OS models, it ships none. Change: Price Macros on the catalog, check-ins, scanning and widgets alone and treat photo logging as a bonus stated with its device requirement on the unlock sheet and the site. Add a risk: 'photo logging unavailable on my phone' refunds. Change 'two on-device models' to 'uses the phone's own Apple Intelligence or Gemini Nano models where present'.
- [medium] '$5 above Caltopia for a catalog 66 times larger' divides 3.2 million Open Food Facts packaged entries by Caltopia's 48,000 USDA foods. Vector Macros bundles 7,793 USDA foods, six times fewer generic foods than Caltopia; the 66x is packaged-product rows of community data whose 'label accuracy ... cannot be guaranteed'. Caltopia is also a second-hand Product Hunt listing whose App Store page the research never located. The line is not one the site could say. Change: Drop the 66x comparison. If a catalog line is wanted, state the two counts plainly ('7,793 USDA foods and 3.19 million packaged foods from 184 countries, offline') with the Open Food Facts attribution, and verify Caltopia on the App Store before using it as an anchor in docs/sources.
- [medium] Lift is priced at $14.99, exactly Benchmark's price, although Benchmark is a logger with no program engine, no Android and no prescription; HeavySet, also a logger, holds 1.3k ratings at $19.99; and Lift's set prescription, travel gyms and plate math are the features of the $79.99 to $95.99 a year band (Alpha Progression, Fitbod). The family order is set by cost to maintain, not by what the buyer gets, so the one app with a clear feature lead over its one-time comparators is the one priced on top of them. The $14.99 'has no comparator to point at' argument cuts the other way: HeavySet at $19.99 is the comparator. Change: Price Lift at $19.99 with the $14.99 launch window as its only concession (ladder $19.99, $19.99, $9.99, $4.99, sum $54.96), or at minimum say in the rationale that $14.99 is a deliberate under-price for an unrated Android build and schedule the $19.99 test at 500 ratings exactly as Macros has one at $24.99.
- [medium] The pre-announced launch price undercuts the proposal's strongest claim, that 'a published, unchanging price is the pricing page's strongest line'. Reviews requested after unlock in the first 30 days come from $14.99 and $9.99 buyers, and the day-31 rise is the 'price went up' review the plan is built to avoid; pre-announcement softens it but does not remove it. Under free-plus-unlock the install burst comes from pre-order and pre-registration anyway, so the discount buys little. Change: Launch at the final prices with no window, or restrict the launch price to codes (Play promo codes exist for one-time products; check whether App Store offer codes now cover non-consumables before relying on them) so the public price never moves. If the window stays, require the final price to be shown on the sheet beside the launch price.
- [medium] Free-plus-unlock with a 14-day trial fits Lift and Macros but not Hydration. Every hydration leader logs water free forever (Waterllama, WaterMinder, Leap Fitness) and the research's recommended line is 'no account, no trial, no ads'; a free water app that stops logging on day 15 draws the 'paywall' one-star the category hands out, and the trial argument ('try the catalog') does not apply to a $4.99 single-purpose app. The fact sheet also shows Hydration has no billing code and device acceptance checks still open, so adding StoreKit and Play Billing to it is the most work for the least revenue ($4.24 net). Change: Sell Hydration paid-upfront at $4.99 (no trial, no billing code, Play Family Library works, ships first) and keep free-plus-unlock for Lift and Macros; decide Body either way. The site line stays honest under both: 'buy once'. Note that a mixed family already forgoes the App Store bundle, which the recommended model forgoes anyway.
- [medium] The paid-upfront fallback promises to 'grandfather by original purchase date in the receipt' when migrating to free-plus-unlock. That works on iOS (AppTransaction.originalPurchaseDate). On Android a paid app has no in-app receipt; distinguishing earlier buyers after the listing goes free needs the legacy Licensing Verification Library, an online check that contradicts the plan's 'ordinary offline use never waits on validation' requirement, and a free listing cannot be reverted if the check fails. Change: State the Android grandfathering mechanism (LVL licence check cached on first online launch after the migration, with a grace path for users who never go online) or drop the 'migrate after the first quarter' fallback on Android and ship it only where it can be honoured.
- [medium] Fee timing is misstated. Apple: 'Your proceeds will be adjusted fifteen (15) days after the end of the fiscal calendar month in which your enrollment is approved', so enrolling 'before the first sale' leaves the launch month at 30 percent; enrolment must be approved at least a month before release. Google Play: under the regional model (EEA, UK, US from 2026-06-30) a new app's purchases are 'new installs' at 10 percent plus 5 percent with no tier enrolment, and the 15 percent tier now mainly governs existing installs; Play's opt-out subscription rise needs '30 days or 60 days, depending on the country/region', not a flat 30. Change: Change launch tactic 6 to 'Small Business Program enrolment approved at least one full fiscal month before release'; state the Play rate for a new app as 10 percent plus a 5 percent billing fee in the regional markets and 15 percent tier elsewhere; correct the Play notice period to 30 or 60 days by region.
- [medium] The OmegaFyt price test only probes upward ($7.99/$49.99 against $9.99/$59.99) from a price already above the RevenueCat annual median ($39.94) for an app with unknown ratings, a forced paywall after setup, English only and no offline mode. Cal AI (368K ratings) sells at $29.99 a year, Lose It and Foodnoms at $39.99. Testing only higher arms cannot find the elastic region below. Change: Run the first test as $39.99 against $49.99 annual (monthly held at $7.99), judged on revenue per paywall view; test $59.99 only if $49.99 wins. Keep the one-variable, new-users-only, four-week rule.
- [low] Arithmetic and wording nits that would be wrong on the site. 'A price-driven buyer switches after eight months' is false for an annual subscriber, who has prepaid 12 months (the switch point is renewal); at $7.99 monthly the Vector pair costs 4.4 months. 'Never above the US price' on Play cannot be kept where templates add VAT. 'Apple Watch app with Live Activities' conflates the watch companion with the iPhone Lock Screen and Dynamic Island countdown. 'Photo comparison' is correctly listed as missing for Body, but Body also lacks export, which the rationale does not say. Change: Say 'an annual subscriber switches at renewal; a monthly subscriber has spent the Vector pair's price in five months'. Replace 'never above the US price' with 'set from the US price by each store's template'. Write 'Apple Watch companion app and an iPhone Live Activity rest timer'. Add 'no export' to Body's rationale.
- [low] Two platform statements are asserted without a verified source and are one-way doors: that Family Sharing, once turned on for a non-consumable, can be turned off (App Store Connect Help says it cannot, but the page could not be fetched this session), and that App Store bundles cannot be pre-ordered (the bundle page read today does not say). Offer codes for non-consumables, which would let the launch price exist without moving the public price, are likewise unverified (Apple's in-app-purchase page describes offer codes only beside subscriptions). Change: Before the owner table is finalised, verify in App Store Connect Help: Family Sharing reversibility for in-app purchases (treat as permanent until shown otherwise), bundle pre-order, and whether offer codes cover non-consumables; record each with URL and date in docs/sources.
